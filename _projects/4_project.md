Superconductivity emerges when electrons form coherent pairs. My research investigates how electronic interactions, orbital character, and lattice geometry select the structure of those pairs. Using first-principles models and many-body calculations, I study alternatives to conventional phonon-mediated pairing and identify signatures that can help experiments distinguish competing superconducting states.

## Pairing on the kagome lattice

In the AV<sub>3</sub>Sb<sub>5</sub> family (A = K, Rb, Cs), superconductivity develops in an electronic structure with several nearby van Hove singularities. Our **Physical Review Letters (2021)** study showed how the distribution of electronic states across kagome sublattices, together with nonlocal Coulomb repulsion, affects the favoured pairing symmetry. This provides a microscopic framework for understanding competing superconducting instabilities rather than assigning a universal pairing state to the entire family.

[Nature of Unconventional Pairing in the Kagome Superconductors AV3Sb5 (A = K, Rb, Cs)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.177001) · *Physical Review Letters* **127**, 177001 (2021).

## Triplet pairing in an atomic tin layer

A triangular layer of tin atoms on silicon offers a setting in which interactions between electrons on neighbouring sites matter strongly. Our **Physical Review Letters (2022)** calculations predict that these nonlocal repulsive interactions can favour spin-triplet pairing: an f-wave state at moderate hole doping and a p-wave state at higher doping within the model studied. This identifies a possible route to unconventional superconductivity in a material platform compatible with semiconductor substrates. The predicted pairing symmetry remains a question for experimental tests.

[Triplet Superconductivity from Nonlocal Coulomb Repulsion in an Atomic Sn Layer Deposited onto a Si(111) Substrate](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.167002) · *Physical Review Letters* **128**, 167002 (2022), Editors' Suggestion.

## Orbital-selective pairing in nickelates

In **Physical Review B (2025)**, we developed a first-principles-guided description of infinite-layer LaNiO<sub>2</sub> that includes both nickel and lanthanum orbitals. The calculations reveal competition between even-parity d-wave and odd-parity p-wave pairing. Strong renormalization of the nickel orbital's quasiparticle weight can favour the p-wave channel, demonstrating how unequal correlation effects across orbitals can change the preferred superconducting state. This is a theoretical mechanism for triplet pairing, not an experimental identification of the order parameter.

[Orbital-selective spin-triplet superconductivity in infinite-layer LaNiO2](https://doi.org/10.1103/bq5m-yw6f) · *Physical Review B* **112**, L100503 (2025).

Across these systems, the central question is how the microscopic electronic structure selects a pairing mechanism and symmetry.
